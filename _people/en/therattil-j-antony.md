To the people of Aranattukara, Therattil J. Antony was simply *Anthonichettan* (elder brother Antony). He was the local face of the Indian National Congress for a generation, and people on all sides respected him.

## Party worker

Even before Kerala was formed in 1956, the book says, the Congress office in Aranattukara worked from a house near the chapel at Padinjare Angadi. He and Thazhath J. Antony were the party's local leaders. Those were hard years, with frequent clashes between Congress and Communist workers.

## Councillor and chairman

In 1962 he and Thazhath J. Antony won seats on the Thrissur Municipal Council. He went on to serve as a councillor for about thirty years. For about fifteen of them he was chairman of the municipality, the "father of the council". The book records him in that office in 1969.

As chairman he kept up friendships across party lines. He brought piped drinking water to the Mathaipuram colony at Lalur. He also championed a Ring Road scheme for Thrissur. It stalled over land acquisition, and the book notes that the city still talks of it as a dream project, while the land that could once have been bought has long since gone up in price.

## A modest man

The book describes him as the biggest leather merchant in the old Cochin State, yet a very simple and humble man. He quietly helped poor families, and he supported both the church and the school in Aranattukara. The lower-primary section of [Tharakans School]({{ '/en/places/tharakans-school/' | relative_url }}) later moved into a building he had paid for.

The book remembers him less for learning than for his honesty and good character.
