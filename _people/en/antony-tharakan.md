The Chiramel Tharakan family runs all through Aranattukara's story, from the boat landing they once controlled to the school that still carries their name. Of all its members, Antony Tharakan is the one the book praises most.

## Physician to the palace

The Tharakans were known for Ayurvedic medicine. Antony Tharakan was remembered as a master of *ottamooli*, remedies made from a single herb. According to the book, the Maharaja of Cochin gave him the title *Vaidyaratnam* ("jewel among physicians") and the rank of *Madampi* (a landed chieftain). He also gave him a seat on the Durbar, the state's governing council, where he was the only Christian member. One passage of the book gives these honours to an elder Antony Tharakan of the same family, so some of them may belong to him. Antony Tharakan is said to have treated the sick without taking money.

A family story shows how people saw him. Fishermen once left a pan of fish at the house, with their backs broken, under a few leafy twigs. When the cooks came, the fish were alive and jumping. The twigs, it turned out, came from a wound-healing herb. The story goes that the remedy joined the family's collection of palm-leaf manuscripts.

## Learning and honours

He cared about schooling. In 1905 he led the rebuilding of Aranattukara's small English school, and the new building opened in 1907 as St. John's School. In 1911 Pope Pius X made him a knight of the Order of St. Sylvester. The book notes that no one in the Thrissur diocese had received the honour before. That is why he is remembered as "Chevalier".

He died in 1912, still in his early thirties, and was buried inside the old East Church near the main altar. His family's house, with its thick sand-filled walls and its own chapel, keeps many reminders of his time. See [Tharakan House]({{ '/en/places/tharakan-house/' | relative_url }}).
