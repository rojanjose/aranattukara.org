Kuriakose Elias Chavara was born at Kainakary in Kuttanad in 1805. He became one of the great reformers of the Syrian Catholic Church in Kerala. He helped found the congregation of Carmelite priests now known as the CMI. The book also credits him with starting the CMC and CTC congregations of sisters. His call for a school beside every church led Christian communities all over Kerala to open schools.

## The meeting in the churchyard

Aranattukara has a special place in his story. He was looking for a site for a monastery in the north, and he chose a lonely island at the western end of Aranattukara parish: Elthuruth. In 1856, the book records, the vicar called a special meeting in the churchyard so that Fr. Chavara could explain his plan. The parishioners raised their hands and waved their shoulder cloths to show they agreed. The elders of several families from the western side promised help.

The monastery was founded in 1858. While the work went on, Fr. Chavara would say Mass at the Aranattukara church before going on to the island. Local tradition links him with much that followed. Bunded *kol* farming (paddy grown in low wetlands, protected by earth banks) began near the monastery in his time. Two old mango trees there are said to date from its founding. Later, the school beside the monastery grew into [St. Aloysius College]({{ '/en/places/st-aloysius-college/' | relative_url }}).

## Saint

In 1861 he stood firm against Mar Rokos, a bishop sent from outside to take control of the Kerala Church. He died in 1871. Pope John Paul II declared him Blessed during his visit to Kerala in February 1986, and he was declared a saint in Rome in November 2014. The book says that Aranattukara counts itself blessed to have had him walk its paths. Today the [Elthuruth monastery]({{ '/en/places/elthuruth-ashram/' | relative_url }}) is the clearest sign of that link.
