Aranattukara's streets were laid out in a grid of rectangles. Parallel roads were joined by cross roads, and most of them were named after the markets, or *angadis*, along them.

## Five markets

Coming in from the [Chunkam (toll gate)]({{ '/en/places/toll-gate/' | relative_url }}) on Kanjani Road, you pass through:

- **Kizhakke Angadi**, the East Market,
- **Valiyangadi**, the Big Market, with [St. Mary's Chapel]({{ '/en/places/st-marys-chapel/' | relative_url }}) at its heart,
- **Padinjare Angadi**, the West Market, with [St. Joseph's Chapel]({{ '/en/places/st-josephs-chapel/' | relative_url }}),
- **Puthenangadi**, the New Market,
- and **Anchangadi**, the "fifth market", whose road became the main road that links the others.

The roads south of Valiyangadi and Padinjare Angadi run past the church and down to the old [kadavu]({{ '/en/places/warehouse-landing/' | relative_url }}).

## The "Tharakan Market"

The book says the Aranattukara *angadi* was famous before Thrissur had a Chettiyangadi, a Nayarangadi or today's Sakthan Thampuran Market. At one time the Tharakans developed it at their own expense, and people called it the "Tharakan Market". It was lined with rows of *aras* (storerooms), so going to market was "going to the *aramukham*", the face of the storerooms.

Goods arrived by bullock cart and as head-loads, sometimes in long lines of carts at night with hurricane lanterns swinging behind them. They went on by boat to Kochi, Kodungallur and Kozhikode.

## Merchants of Aranattukara

The book describes Aranattukara's Nasrani families as traders known across Kerala, in salt, tobacco, leather, groceries and hill produce, elephant caparisons, silk parasols and church vestments. Even the grocery merchants of Thrissur's Nayarangadi and Chettiyangadi, it says, were all from Aranattukara. The profits built the two- and three-storeyed tiled houses that still stand here and there along the market streets. You can read more in [Work and Industry]({{ '/en/stories/work-and-industry/' | relative_url }}).

## The markets today

The cargo boats have gone, but the market streets are still full of shops: bakeries, a meat stall, groceries, medical shops and workshops, between old shop rows with balconies and newer concrete buildings. Kurian's Bakers, an early bakery with its own *borma* (oven), started in Valiyangadi. Another oven bakery still works in the market.
