Until the late 1940s Aranattukara had no high school for girls. The Infant Jesus Girls' High School changed that.

## Beginnings

The school grew out of the [Infant Jesus Convent]({{ '/en/places/infant-jesus-convent/' | relative_url }}). When Tharakan's School became a high school in 1947–48, its girl pupils were handed over to the Holy Family sisters, who taught them in a rented house at Padinjare Angadi. In 1950 these classes became a separate girls' high school, built by the sisters beside their new convent on the western side of Padinjare Angadi. (The book gives the year as 1950 in one place and 1950–51 in another.) A lower primary section followed in 1952–53.

## A school with a name

The book praises the school for its pass rates and for its activities outside the classroom. Much of the credit goes to its headmistress, Sister Ezhsala, whose work was honoured with the National Teacher Award in 1971.

In the years when the parish was divided between two churches, the girls of both sides studied here together. The book counts the school, along with St. John's and Tharakan's, among the places where that division began to heal.
