Calvary Ashram is the house of the Capuchins, a branch of the Franciscan order, at Poothole in Aranattukara. Today it belongs to the Capuchins' St. Thomas Province of Kerala, and it trains young friars in philosophy.

## How it began

Bishop George Alappatt of Thrissur invited the Capuchins to his diocese. On 10 May 1954, Chevalier Inchodikkaran Iyyappan gave them land at Poothole for a nominal price. The book records that it was 72 acres at one *anna* a cent. The land, known as *Kollipparambu*, had earlier belonged to the Koola family of Kuruthukulangara. He also helped generously with building the chapel.

The foundation stone of the chapel was laid on 27 February 1955, and Bishop Alappatt blessed the chapel on 14 September 1958. Both the friary and the chapel were built by one of the friars, Br. Athanasius Cheruvil.

## Work among the people

The friars were known for retreats and for going from house to house with the cross, praying with families. The book credits them with helping to end many old superstitions. Their leadership camps for young people trained many who later became prominent in Thrissur district. The ashram is not a parish, but it has long run catechism classes and lay associations such as the Catholic Labour Association and the Assisi Samajam.

Its social work began early. The friars started the Nirmala Dress Manufacturing Company in 1960, and the book records a social centre, opened in 1961, that gave poor women paid work, from making umbrellas to rolling *beedis* (the *beedi* work was later stopped). The Calvary Poor Fund and Fr. Claude Charities (1988) followed. The ashram also became a home for drama: under the banner Assisi Arts, the friars staged Bible plays and social plays.

## A house of studies

In 1960 the friary became a formal house of training, and a block for student friars was blessed on 31 May 1961. In 1972 it became one of the shared philosophy colleges for the Capuchins of all four Indian provinces. From 1981 to 1986 it also taught theology, until that moved to Kottayam. Since 1999 the St. Thomas Province has run the college, which trains the student friars of the three Syro-Malabar Capuchin provinces. It was renamed the Calvary Institute of Philosophy and Religion in 2021. New wings, a library (2007) and other buildings were added over the years.

The friars' cemetery dates from 1967. The book describes the ashram as a lively presence in Aranattukara to this day, with worship, care for the poor and social work.
