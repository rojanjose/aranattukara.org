In the market streets stands an old two-storey building with an upstairs veranda behind turned balusters, slim pillars at its entrance porch and a small cross on the gable. Its roof is now covered with blue sheets. This is the old building of Aranattukara's co-operative bank. Today it looks out over a patch of grass.

## Aranattukara's own banks

Banking came early to Aranattukara. As trade grew, so did a class of moneylenders, and the book says local banks were started to free the traders from them.

- In **1919** leading merchants started the **Aranattukara Catholic Oriental Bank**. Its directors were Arimpur Chovvookkaran Varunni Ouseph, Thathrathil Ponmani Porinju, Poothokkaran Kunjippalu, Chittilappilly Kunjappu Antony and Chungath Rappayi Joseph.
- The same year the **Aranattukara Union Bank** was registered. It ran mainly *kuri* (chit funds). The first draw of each chit went to the bank and the fiftieth to the parish church, so the church and its people backed it.
- The Catholic Social Bank and the Vidyabhivardhini Sangham Bank also worked here.

When the Reserve Bank of India pushed small banks to merge, both 1919 banks were taken into the South Indian Bank. Their chit business carried on as **Aranattukara Kuries**.

The Aranattukara Oriental Co-operative Bank now has its office on St. Thomas Nagar Road; you can find it and the other banks in the [Business Directory]({{ '/en/business/' | relative_url }}). For more on the merchants who started these banks, see [The Aranattukara Markets]({{ '/en/places/aranattukara-market/' | relative_url }}).
