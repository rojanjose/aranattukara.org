In Kerala a small *kappela* (wayside chapel) often stands a little way from the parish church, a place where passers-by stop, light a candle and say a prayer. St. Mary's Chapel is Aranattukara's best known. It stands beside the main road in the middle of Valiyangadi, at the spot now called Fatima Corner.

## An old chapel, rebuilt

People believe the first chapel here is as old as the parish church itself. Tradition says it was built by a local master builder, Miyyalutty Mesthri, whose name was once carved on a plaque.

Early in the twentieth century the chapel was rebuilt by Chungath Rappai Thomakutty, then a lay trustee of the church. The book credits him with casting the first reinforced concrete beam in Aranattukara, at a time when builders still made arches of laterite. More than a century later, the chapel is still sound.

## Inside

The chapel keeps a picture of Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception, seated in a chair with the infant Jesus on her lap. Its main celebration marks the end of the *Vanakkamasam* (the month of Marian devotion), when the chapel is lit up and *pachoru* (sweet rice porridge) is shared as an offering.

The book calls the chapel a witness to the years when the parish was divided, and to the day the quarrel ended.
