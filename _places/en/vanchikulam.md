For centuries Thrissur's goods reached the backwaters through the [kadavu (boat landing) at Aranattukara]({{ '/en/places/warehouse-landing/' | relative_url }}). Vanchikulam, a short way to the east, is where that trade went next.

## Where the boats moved

The book tells the story in two steps. First the railway reached Thrissur, and goods began to travel by train. Then the Puthenthodu canal was dug through as far as Kokkala, and the cargo boats stopped coming to Aranattukara. They tied up instead at the canal basin now called Vanchikulam (also written Vanchikkulam). The old landing silted up, while Vanchikulam became Thrissur's inland port for the last years of water transport.

That did not last long either. Lorries and buses took over, and the boats stopped coming here too.

## What you can see today

The basin is still there, with stone steps leading down to the water. Along the road stand long two-storey warehouses with wooden upper floors. Their ground floors are now shops. At the water's edge is a big open shed with a tiled roof, and a large mural covers its end wall. The space has been laid out as a public square with paving, an entrance pavilion and a sculpture. It is used for gatherings. The poster at the entrance in November 2026 was for the Vanchikulam Fest of 21–23 February 2025, three days of art, music, talks, films and stalls.

You can read the whole story of Aranattukara's trade by water in [The Port of Thrissivaperur]({{ '/en/stories/port-of-thrissivaperur/' | relative_url }}).
