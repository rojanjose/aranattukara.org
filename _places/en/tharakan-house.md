The Chiramel Tharakan family, the first Syrian Catholic family to settle in Aranattukara according to the book, lived close to the *kadavu* (boat landing). Their *tharavadu* (ancestral home) still stands as one of the most important landmarks of the area's past.

## Close to the water

The family took its title from service to the Cochin kings, and for a long time the Tharakan controlled the buying and selling at the landing. Boat steps once led from the water to the family's houses; the book notes that these landings have all been cut off.

## Inside the house

The book gives a rich picture of the old drawing room. Its double wall, about thirty-two inches thick, is filled with sand, a defence against the torch-carrying robber gangs known as *theevetti*: break through it and only sand pours out. The book lists crystal lamps, a grain chest about twelve feet long hollowed from a single log, plates with a hollow for hot water to keep food warm, doors with secret catches, the robe of Antony Tharakan's papal knighthood, a *Pietà* painted with leaf dyes, and palm-leaf manuscripts of *ottamooli* (single-herb remedies) from the family's long tradition of Ayurvedic medicine.

The family also had its own house chapel, which the book describes as a rare privilege granted by the Pope. That chapel has since been taken down, but many of its statues and fittings survive.

## Please note

This is a private family home, not a museum. Please view it only from the road unless the family invites you in.
